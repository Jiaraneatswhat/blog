<i>Everyone moans about the length of their commute. Should managers care?</i>
</span>

<div style="height:30px;"></div>

    <source src="https://audio.taffybook.cn/Economist/2026-05/065%20Business%20-%20Bartleby.mp3">

<span style="font-family: Gulliver;">ASK SOMEONE if they have regrets, and very few people will say: “I wish I had spent more of my life commuting.” The time spent travelling from home to work and back again tends to be neither relaxing nor productive. It is usually routine and sometimes unpleasant: anything that involves loads of traffic or armpits is hard to like.</span>

<font color="#548dd4">随便问一个人有无遗憾，几乎没人会说：“真希望这辈子多花点时间通勤。”往返上下班的路途时光，既无法放松身心，也做不了正事。这段行程向来枯燥乏味，有时还令人糟心：遇上拥堵车流、拥挤人群，实在让人提不起好感。</font>

<span style="font-family: Gulliver;">In popular culture the monotony is the point. Commuting becomes a trap (“Exit 8”), a window on the real action (“The Girl on the Train”) or a commentary on the softness of modern society (“The War of the Worlds”). The covid-19 pandemic gave people the chance to experience a commute-free existence, and many of them loved it.</span>

<font color="#548dd4">在大众文化作品里，通勤的枯燥乏味本就是创作立意所在。通勤或是沦为桎梏牢笼（《 </font>$\textcolor{#548dd4}{8}$ <font color="#548dd4"> 号出口》），或是窥见世事百态的窗口（《火车上的女孩》），亦或是用来讽刺现代社会安逸颓靡的缩影（《世界大战》）。新冠疫情让人们过上了无需通勤的日子，不少人对此倍感惬意。</font>

<span style="font-family: Gulliver;">So when people are surveyed about their perfect commute time, the unsurprising answer tends to be “shorter”. On average people around the world spend roughly an hour a day commuting. But however long their travel time is, they want to <mark style="background:#fdbfff">lop</mark>[^1] that total roughly in half.</span>

<font color="#548dd4">因此当被问及理想通勤时长时，答案不出所料 ——越短越好。全球民众日均通勤时长约一小时，无论实际通勤多久，大家都希望把总时长砍掉将近一半。</font>

<span style="font-family: Gulliver;">A study published last year by Jonas De Vos of University College London into the commuting preferences of over 2,000 students and staff at the university is typical. The average actual commute time was a <mark style="background:#fdbfff">hefty</mark>[^2] 54 minutes one way; the average ideal time was 31 minutes. (Ideals vary depending on how someone gets to work: a person who is on a train for an hour, for example, envisages a commute that is much longer than someone with a 30-minute bike ride.)</span>

<font color="#548dd4">伦敦大学学院的乔纳斯・德沃斯去年发布了一项研究，调研校内两千余名师生的通勤偏好，调研结果颇具代表性。受访者实际单程通勤时长足足有</font> $\textcolor{#548dd4}{54}$ <font color="#548dd4">分钟，而大家心中理想时长仅为</font> $\textcolor{#548dd4}{31}$ <font color="#548dd4">分钟。理想通勤时长也因出行方式而异：比如搭乘一小时火车通勤的人，心中预想的合理通勤时长，会远高于骑行三十分钟上下班的人。</font>

<span style="font-family: Gulliver;">Long journeys can impose costs on employers as well as commuters. For instance, they can increase staff turnover. A paper by Francisco Santelli of Brown University and Jason Grissom of Vanderbilt University examines the commute times for teachers at public schools serving Nashville, Tennessee.They find that each five-minute increase in one-way commute time predicts a 0.8-1 percentage-point increase in the probability that a teacher will transfer to another school within the district.</span>

<font color="#548dd4">长途通勤不仅给上班族带来负担，也会让雇主蒙受损失，人员流动率上升便是其一。布朗大学弗朗西斯科・桑特利与范德堡大学杰森・格里森姆联合发文，调研了田纳西州纳什维尔公立学校教师的通勤时长。研究发现，单程通勤每增加五分钟，教师在学区内调任其他学校的概率就会上升</font> $\textcolor{#548dd4}{0.8}$ <font color="#548dd4">至</font> $\textcolor{#548dd4}{1}$ <font color="#548dd4">个百分点。</font>

<span style="font-family: Gulliver;">Time spent travelling can also hurt productivity. A study by Hongyu Xiao of the Bank of Canada and his co-authors found that every 10km increase in an inventor’s commuting distance brought about by a corporate relocation was associated with a 5% decrease in patents. What lies behind this fall-off is not clear, but one explanation is simply that people are spending less time at work.</span>

<font color="#548dd4">通勤耗时过长还会拉低工作效率。加拿大银行肖宏宇及其合著者开展的一项研究发现，企业搬迁致使研发人员通勤距离每增加</font> $\textcolor{#548dd4}{10}$ <font color="#548dd4">公里，其专利产出量就会下降</font> $\textcolor{#548dd4}{5\%}$<font color="#548dd4">。目前这种产出下滑的深层原因尚无定论，一种合理解释是员工在岗工作时长随之变少。</font>

<span style="font-family: Gulliver;">No one yearns to spend more of their life commuting, then. But even long journeys can have benefits. In his study, for example, Mr De Vos also asked people what was the maximum commute length they were prepared to tolerate. This was more, on average, than the length of their current commute, which suggests some awareness of the upsides to tolerating <mark style="background:#fdbfff">protracted</mark>[^3] travel times: lower housing costs, better <mark style="background:#fdbfff">amenities</mark>[^4] close to home and a greater choice of employer.</span>

<font color="#548dd4">由此看来，没人愿意把大把人生耗费在通勤路上。不过漫长通勤也并非全无益处。例如德沃斯在研究中还询问了受访者所能忍受的最长通勤时长，大家给出的平均耐受时长，普遍比自己当下实际通勤时间更长。这也说明人们心里清楚，忍受远距离通勤自有好处：住房开销更低、居家周边配套更完善、择业选择也更加宽泛。</font>

<span style="font-family: Gulliver;">Ismir Mulalic of Copenhagen Business School points out that the one-hour global average commute has remained pretty steady over time despite big changes in transport technology. He recalls a study he co-authored into the effects of a corporate relocation; to his surprise, some of the workers who were suddenly handed shorter commutes reacted to this “beautiful present” by moving home to be farther away.</span>

<font color="#548dd4">哥本哈根商学院的伊斯米尔・穆拉利奇指出，尽管交通技术日新月异，全球一小时的日均通勤时长长期以来始终保持稳定。他曾参与一项企业搬迁影响研究，令他意外的是，部分员工骤然缩短通勤路程后，竟把这份 “美事” 当作契机，反倒搬去了离家更远的住处。</font>

<span style="font-family: Gulliver;">A commute can bring rewards of its own, too. Some scholars <mark style="background:#fdbfff">burble</mark>[^5] about commuting as “a liminal space”, a <mark style="background:#fdbfff">pompous</mark>[^6] term that nonetheless captures something real: the journey to and from the office is a handy way to gear up for the working day or to wind down from it. Active modes of travel like cycling and walking are correlated with greater well-being than sitting in a car.</span>

<font color="#548dd4">通勤本身也能带来别样收获。一些学者津津乐道，将通勤称作阈限空间，这个略显浮夸的说法却道出实情：往返职场的路途，恰好能让人调整状态，从容开启一日工作，或是舒缓情绪卸下工作疲惫。比起驾车出行，骑行、步行这类主动出行方式更能提升身心幸福感。</font>

<span style="font-family: Gulliver;">Firms are right not to be indifferent to their employees’ journeys to work. Plenty offer explicit and implicit subsidies to <mark style="background:#fdbfff">defray</mark>[^7] commuting costs, from financial help to parking spaces. Hybrid working and flexible hours can also ease the burden of travelling. But the greatest power to improve commute times, through better public transport and more housing supply, lies in the hands of policymakers. And to the extent that commuting time is an expression of individual preferences, firms should pay most attention when those preferences suddenly change.</span>

<font color="#548dd4">企业重视员工通勤问题实属明智之举。不少公司会以明里暗里的补贴分担通勤开销，既有资金补助，也提供停车场地。混合办公与弹性工时同样能减轻出行压力。但想要完善公共交通、增加住房供给以优化通勤时长，最大主动权仍掌握在政策制定者手中。而通勤时长本质也体现着个人选择倾向，一旦这类倾向发生突变，企业便需格外留意。</font>

<span style="font-family: Gulliver;">That means one event in particular. A paper co-authored by Mr Mulalic which looked at data for the full working population of Denmark between 2003 and 2013 found that women with a long commute are several times more likely to change jobs when they have a child. (Men are not.) Everyone says they want a shorter commute. New mothers mean it.</span>

<font color="#548dd4">这一点在特定情况中尤为明显。穆拉利奇参与撰写的一篇论文，分析了</font> $\textcolor{#548dd4}{2003}$ <font color="#548dd4">至</font> $\textcolor{#548dd4}{2013}$ <font color="#548dd4">年丹麦全体在职人群数据后发现：通勤路程远的女性，生育后跳槽换工作的概率会成倍增加，而男性则无此明显变化。人人都说希望缩短通勤时长，新晋妈妈们对此更是由衷迫切。</font>
