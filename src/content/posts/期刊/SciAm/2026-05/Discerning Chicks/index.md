<p style="margin:0!important;text-indent:0!important;font-family: 'Gulliver' !important;"><span style="background:#000000;color:#ffffff;font-weight:bold;padding:0 4px;user-select:none;">COGNITION</span></p>

<span style="font-family: Gulliver;">**WHY DOES “BOUBA” SOUND ROUND** and “kiki” sound spiky? This intuition that ties certain sounds to shapes is oddly reliable all over the world. For at least a century scientists have considered this reliability a clue to the origin of language, theorizing that maybe our ancestors built their first words on these instinctive associations between sound and meaning. But a study published recently in *Science* adds an unexpected twist: baby chickens make these same sound-shape connections, which suggests the link to human language may not be unique. If a barnyard hen agrees with our innate connections between shapes and sounds, you might wonder whether we’ve been pecking at the wrong linguistic seed.</span>

<font color="#548dd4">为什么 “布巴” 听起来圆润，而 “奇奇” 听起来尖锐？这种将特定发音与形状关联的直觉，在全球范围内都出奇地一致。至少一个世纪以来，科学家都把这种共性视作语言起源的线索，并提出理论：人类祖先最初创造词汇时，或许正是依托这种声音与含义之间与生俱来的关联。但《科学》杂志近期发表的一项研究带来了出人意料的新发现：雏鸡也具备这种声音-形状关联能力。这意味着，这种关联并非人类语言所独有。倘若家禽母鸡也和人类一样天生能感知声音与形状的对应关系，我们不禁会怀疑：人类此前探寻语言起源的方向，或许从一开始就找错了根源。</font>

![](./images/fig.png "w-30")

<span style="font-family: Gulliver;">Maria Loconsole, a comparative psychologist at the University of Padua in Italy, and her colleagues decided to investigate the so-called bouba-kiki effect in chicks because the birds could be tested almost immediately after hatching, before their brain could be influenced by exposure to the world. The researchers placed chicks in front of two panels: one featured a flowerlike shape with gently rounded curves; the other had a spiky <mark style="background:#fdbfff">blotch</mark>[^1] <mark style="background:#fdbfff">reminiscent</mark>[^2] of a cartoon explosion. They then played recordings of humans saying either “bouba” or “kiki” and observed the birds’ behavior. When the chicks heard “bouba,” 80 percent of them approached the round shape first and spent an average of more than three minutes exploring it, compared with an average of just under one minute spent exploring the spiky shape. The exploration preferences flipped when the chicks heard “kiki.”</span>

<font color="#548dd4">意大利帕多瓦大学比较心理学家玛丽亚・洛孔索莱及其团队，决定在雏鸡身上研究所谓的布巴-奇奇效应。选择雏鸡的原因是：可以在它们刚孵化后立刻开展测试，此时其大脑尚未受外界环境接触的影响。研究人员将雏鸡置于两块展板前：一块上面是线条柔和、呈花朵状的圆润曲线图形；另一块则是棱角突兀的斑驳图案，类似卡通里爆炸的形状。随后研究人员播放人类念出 “布巴” 或 “奇奇” 的录音，并观察雏鸡的行为反应。当雏鸡听到 “布巴” 时，80% 会首先走向圆形图案，且平均会花三分钟以上探究该图形；而它们在棱角图形上停留探究的平均时间不足一分钟。当雏鸡听到 “奇奇” 时，偏好倾向则完全相反。</font>

<span style="font-family: Gulliver;">Because the tests took place within the chicks’ carefully supervised first hours of life outside their eggshell, this association between particular sounds and shapes couldn’t have been learned from experience. Instead it may be evidence of an innate perceptual bias that goes back much further in our evolutionary history than previously believed.</span>

<font color="#548dd4">由于这些实验是在雏鸡破壳而出、来到世间的最初几小时内，且在严格监控下完成的，这种特定声音与形状之间的关联不可能是后天经验习得的。相反，这或许证明了一种先天感知偏好，其演化起源比我们以往所认为的要久远得多。</font>

<span style="font-family: Gulliver;">“We parted with birds on the evolutionary line 300 million years ago,” says Aleksandra Cwiek, a linguist at Nicolaus Copernicus University in Toruń, Poland, who was not involved in the study. “It’s just mind-blowing.”</span>

<font color="#548dd4">波兰托伦哥白尼大学语言学家亚历山德拉・茨维克并未参与这项研究，她表示：“人类和鸟类在演化谱系上早在 3 亿年前就分道扬镳了，这实在令人震惊。”</font>

<span style="font-family: Gulliver;">In a 2022 paper, Cwiek and her colleagues demonstrated that the bouba-kiki effect held across diverse cultures and writing systems worldwide. Other experiments have found that human infants perform similarly on these tests, even before they’ve learned to speak. In 2019 and 2022 researchers tested the effect in great apes and found that they failed the bouba-kiki test, which suggested that the effect might be exclusive to humans and our linguistic capabilities.</span>

<font color="#548dd4">茨维克及其团队在 2022 年发表的论文中证实，布巴-奇奇效应在全球不同文化、不同书写体系中都普遍成立。另有多项实验发现，人类婴儿即便尚未学会说话，在这类测试中也会表现出相同的本能倾向。2019 年和 2022 年，研究人员在类人猿身上开展该效应测试，结果发现类人猿无法通过布巴–奇奇测试。这也一度让人认为，该效应或许为人类独有，与人类的语言能力紧密相关。</font>

<span style="font-family: Gulliver;">Loconsole argues that the apes’ prior communicative training might have skewed their performance. Jared Taglialatela, director of the Ape Initiative research center in Iowa and co-author of one of the ape studies, agrees. That study’s subject, Kanzi the bonobo, who recently passed away, was often given similar language-related tests. It’s possible that when Kanzi encountered these new nonsense words, he tried to guess their “meaning” rather than following his gut association.</span>

<font color="#548dd4">洛孔索莱认为，类人猿此前接受过的交流训练，或许干扰、偏离了它们在测试中的真实表现。爱荷华州类人猿研究中心负责人贾里德・塔利亚泰拉，也是其中一项类人猿相关研究的合著者，他对此表示认同。该研究的实验对象是倭黑猩猩坎兹（现已离世），它此前经常接受各类与语言相关的测试。很有可能，当坎兹面对这些无实际意义的新词时，它会刻意去揣测这些词的 “含义”，而非遵从本能的直觉联想。</font>

<span style="font-family: Gulliver;">In light of the new chick findings, Cwiek has also taken a  broader view. “It actually makes the question of bouba-kiki being a solution to language evolution less interesting because it is prelanguage,” she says. “I  think it shows us something deeper about cognition, about the capacity for connecting senses.”</span>

<font color="#548dd4">基于雏鸡实验的新发现，茨维克也提出了更宏观的看法。她说：“这反倒让‘布巴-奇奇效应是语言演化的一种成因’这一说法没那么有说服力了，因为这种声形关联早在语言诞生之前就已存在。我认为它向我们揭示了更深层的认知规律，即生物本身具备跨感官建立关联的本能能力。”</font>

<span style="font-family: Gulliver;">As for what on Earth makes “bouba” round and “kiki” spiky, we may be able to rule out one long-standing theory: that these associations are inspired by the shape your mouth makes when you say each word. The “b” sound does require rounding your lips, and the “k” sound calls for an explosive tongue tap to the roof of your mouth, but obviously chickens can’t say these words at all. Instead the bouba-kiki effect may stem from the physical properties of objects, as some researchers have suggested. When round objects hit the ground or roll, they typically produce more continuous, low-frequency sounds than spiky ones. A built-in grasp of those dynamics, linking sight and sound, could help newborn animals quickly make sense of their environment, possibly to locate food or avoid predators.</span>

<font color="#548dd4">至于究竟是什么原因，让 “布巴” 听感圆润、“奇奇” 听感尖锐，我们或许可以排除一个由来已久的经典理论：这种声形联想，源自人们发这两个词时嘴巴做出的形状。发 /b/ 音确实需要双唇拢圆，发 /k/ 音也需要舌头快速弹贴上颚、形成爆破音，但显然小鸡根本无法发出这些音节。正如部分研究者所提出的，布巴-奇奇效应或许源于物体本身的物理属性。圆形物体落地或滚动时，通常会比棱角尖锐的物体发出更绵长、低频的声音。生物与生俱来对这类物理规律的感知，能够联通视觉与听觉，帮助新生动物快速适应周遭环境，或许借此觅食、躲避天敌。</font>

<span style="font-family: Gulliver;">The bouba-kiki effect might have played a role in the emergence of language, along with many other cognitive faculties. But for chickens (and presumably other animals), these same predispositions seem to serve a more evolutionarily ancient purpose. “Even if language [is] unique to humans,” Loconsole says, “that doesn’t mean that it comes from an ability that is unique to humans.”</span>

<font color="#548dd4">布巴-奇奇效应或许和许多其他认知能力一样，在语言的诞生过程中起到了一定作用。但对于雏鸡（以及大概率其他动物）而言，这类先天倾向有着更为古老的演化意义。洛孔索莱表示：“即便语言为人类所独有，也并不意味着，构成语言的底层能力是人类专属的。”</font>

<p style="font-family: 'Gulliver' !important;" align="right">—<i>Cody Cottier</i></p>
